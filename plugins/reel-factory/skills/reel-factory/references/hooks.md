# Hook, re-hook and CTA library

Always draft **5 hooks** from different formulas, score each 1–5 on *(specific, surprising, relevant to the target viewer, visualisable in 1 s, true)*, and use the winner. Keep the runner-up as the on-screen `hookTitle` if it adds information.

**Visual hook pairings:** a kinetic `title` building word by word with one `[box]` or `*em*` word; the result shown immediately (`image`/`terminal`); a famous *logo* (never a person's likeness); a big `stat` count-up; a `grid` preview of N items.

**Added from batch-02 (2026-11-12):**
- **Disruption claim, resolved fast:** "Claude just killed web designers." Seen with 2 creators (MotionSites, AIS). Pay it off within 5 s with the real reason ("…your setup can build the same sites"), or it reads as clickbait.
- **Insider-secret question:** "Did you know the best designers use GitHub as their asset library?"
- **Wrong way → right way:** "Many people just Google their keyword. What you actually have to do is…" (local-SEO heatmaps).
- **"This is probably why…":** "This is probably why your new page isn't showing up in Google. And the fix is simple." Diagnosis + relief in one breath.
- **End open loop (retention for 60–100 s reels):** "Stay to the end and I'll show you how to build one in under 60 seconds" / "…I'll give you a tool that does it." Only if the end delivers it.
- **Series tag:** "Day 1 of…" / "DEBUG WITHOUT DEPLOY · 07" under the title builds a habit (follow CTA).

**Hook killers (never):** attribution first ("An Anthropic mathematician says…", "According to Reuters…"; put the source on screen and the emotion or stake in the first words), a first sentence over ~12 words or three facts in one breath (attention.md §1), greetings, channel intros, "in today's video", "let me tell you about", starting with the product name and no outcome, a question with an obvious answer, a hook the reel doesn't pay off, a title that promises something else than the content (batch-02 "lifetime free AI tools" over animation libraries).

## The opening lock: stop → amplify → commit (first ~4–11 s, every narrated reel)

Across the corpus (80 hooks, plus the batch-03 openings transcribed 2026-11-16), the reels that hold viewers don't rely on one hook line. Whatever the topic, the first seconds make **three moves**:

1. **STOP (0–4 s):** one specific, surprising, true statement that gives the viewer a reason to stop scrolling. It is the *outcome, consequence or tension*, never the background, a date or a topic announcement.
   - **News = the NEWEST development in the first words (owner hard rule, 2026-11-25).** Lead with what just happened ("Nvidia just brought Jensen Huang back as CEO…"), then attach the older event as the amplifier ("…only eleven days after he quit over an affair with an alleged Chinese spy"). Never open on an earlier event the viewer may already know is outdated ("Jensen Huang quit Nvidia…" when he is already back). Viewers who know the newer state judge the reel stale in the first second and skip. Test: if the first 5 words were the whole reel, would they report today's news?
2. **AMPLIFY (≈2–8 s):** makes the stop bigger, more personal or more credible: the cost removed, a proof number, a precedent, an irony, the stakes for *you*, an authority. It is often fused into S1 with ", and…" / "because…" / "after…".
3. **COMMIT (by ≈11 s):** an explicit or visual promise of what the next seconds deliver, which turns a stop into a watch.
   - Spoken: "Here's what happened." · "Here's how it works." · "…how to use them in 60 seconds." · "Let me show you." · "Save this for later." · "I bet you know fewer than 10."
   - Visual: a preview grid, empty numbered slots, a counter, a before→after split.

Short reels (≤ 30 s) may compress all three into one or two sentences. Long reels (60 s+) need all three spelled out.

### Rules (all reel types)
1. **STOP is the most surprising true thing for the target viewer, not the most important fact.**
   - The result, not the tool: "makes any screen recording look like you hired a motion designer".
   - The consequence, not the event: "his own employees are fighting to bring him back".
   - The pain, not the topic: "your app is slow and you don't know it".

   Name something the viewer recognises: a big brand, a famous person, the product they use, or their own habit. Put a number or stake in S1 or S2.
2. **AMPLIFY adds a second reason, not a repeat:** free or cost removed, a proof number, authority ("Google's own engineers"), precedent ("we've seen this before"), irony ("the best model refused, the open one did it"), or stakes for the viewer.
3. **COMMIT once, then deliver exactly that.**
   - A promised story needs chronology; a promised list needs all N; a promised "how" needs steps.
   - A commitment the reel doesn't keep is a hook killer.
   - In fast reels the commitment can be purely visual (preview grid, empty slots, counter, before→after).
4. **Picture and words land together.** At 0 s the screen shows exactly what S1 names: the result, the post or headline, the footage, the person's real post, or the before→after.
   - With a presenter, he delivers S1 on camera (eyebrows, lean), takes a beat before AMPLIFY and looks straight at camera on COMMIT, with the proof beside or above him.
5. **Language:**
   - Spoken and fast (≈3.5 words/s); emotionally loaded but true words ("insane", "just", "finally", "not a joke", "completely free").
   - Concrete numbers said aloud. Dates go on screen as labels, not in S1.
   - Never a greeting, a channel intro, "today we'll talk about", or a lone topic name.
6. **Truth rules apply to the hook first:** sourced claims, allegation language for people, and no overclaim you can't pay off within 5 s.

**Method:** draft **5 complete openings** (STOP + AMPLIFY + COMMIT), each from a different row or formula above. Score each as a unit, 1–5, on surprise, specificity, viewer relevance, visual at 0 s, truth and commitment strength. Read the winner aloud: S1 should land by ~4 s and the commitment by ~11 s (≤ 6 s for reels under 30 s).

## Re-hook phrases (place at 40–60%)

"But here's the smart part." · "And here's the trick." · "Now this next one is huge." · "But that's not even the most interesting part." · "OK, but this is the fun part." · "Now here's where it gets crazy." · "But here's the catch." · "Does that claim actually hold up?"

## Transitions inside lists

"First… / Second… / And finally…" · "Next up…" · "Number three is the one I use every day." · "The last one is the shortcut."

## Honesty beats

"It's still early, so expect a few bugs." · "If you're just chatting, skip it — this only helps with tool-heavy sessions." · "You'll still need to test with real customers." · "Read what a skill can run before you install it." · "Nobody has publicly proven X."

## CTA patterns

| Type | When | Template | Engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comment keyword → DM (~55%) | Tools, lists, resources (a link or guide exists) | "Comment **WORD** and I'll send you the link / all N links / the full setup." Keyword = the topic word, UPPERCASE, one word. | `cta` kind `comment` |
| Withheld bonus | Lists | "…and I'll send all 20, including the ones for sound and physics." | `cta` + `line` |
| Save / screenshot | Cheat sheets, checklists | "Save this for your next launch." / "Screenshot this." | `cta` kind `save` or a final `list` |
| Share-to-friend | Niche-useful | "Send this to a friend who's about to launch." | `cta` kind `save` + `line` |
| Question | Comparisons, frameworks | "Which one would you use? / Which first — comment a number." | `title` |
| Series follow | Series | "Follow so you don't miss Day 6." | `cta` kind `follow` |
| Follow promise | News, series, developing stories | "Follow, because the moment X happens, I'll break down what changes." A concrete next episode, never "for more content". | `cta` kind `follow` |
| Callback + verdict | News, story twist endings | The last line echoes the first (loop), then the one-line CTA | `title` / `punch` |

The comment-keyword CTA implies the account owner will send links by DM (manual or ManyChat-style automation). If that's not set up, prefer save/share or "link in the caption/bio". Add "follow first so my DM reaches you" (it turns commenters into followers). Every reel also carries the post kit (`engagement`, attention.md §4): caption question about the viewer, pinned comment, named share target, follow promise, 3 story frames.
